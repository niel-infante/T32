> **Project Description: Spatial Mapping of Intercellular Communication Networks in Pathogen-Exposed Tissues**

Multicellular organisms depend on precise cell-to-cell communication — mediated through ligand-receptor interactions, miRNAs, secreted factors, and extracellular matrix signaling — to coordinate development, immunity, and tissue repair. When pathogens such as viruses invade host tissue, they do not simply infect individual cells in isolation; they disrupt the molecular dialogue between cells, reprogramming entire signaling neighborhoods. Understanding _where_ these communication breakdowns occur, and _which_ molecular pathways are hijacked, is a fundamental biochemical and molecular biology question with direct implications for antiviral and immunological research.


Coordinated cell–cell communication (CCC) is fundamental to tissue homeostasis, immune defense, and pathological response. At its molecular core, CCC is mediated through ligand-receptor (LR) interactions that, upon receptor activation, trigger intracellular signaling cascades governing cell behavior. When pathogens such as viruses invade host tissue, they do not merely infect isolated cells — they disrupt the molecular dialogue between cells, rewiring signaling networks across entire tissue neighborhoods. Characterizing _where_ these disruptions occur, and _which_ LR interactions are hijacked or silenced, is a fundamental question in biochemistry and molecular biology with direct implications for understanding viral pathogenesis and host immune response.

Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics (ST) have transformed our ability to study CCC with tissue-level resolution. Unlike bulk or single-cell RNA sequencing, ST preserves the spatial architecture of gene expression, enabling inference of communication between cells that are physically proximal — a critical consideration given that many signaling interactions are inherently local. The growing ecosystem of CCC inference tools, now numbering over 100 as catalogued by Cesaro et al. (2025), provides a powerful but complex analytical landscape. These tools vary widely in their scoring strategies, ligand-receptor databases, and capacity to handle ST data; only a subset — including CellChat, COMMOT, LIANA+, and spaTalk — natively support spatially resolved transcriptomics inputs.
